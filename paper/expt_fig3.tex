\paragraph{Notes on Figure~\ref{fig:min_val_heatmap}.}
At $E = 1$, the entire $4 \times 4$ grid sits in $[4.45, 4.56]$ --- almost flat across a $24 \times$ range in non-embedding parameters.
By $E = 5$ the same grid drops to $[4.17, 4.31]$.
The optimal cell shifts with $E$: at $E = 1$ the smallest non-degenerate cell ($d{=}12, w{=}384$) wins; at $E = 5$ the optimum sits in the middle ($d{=}12, w{=}768$ for both strategies).
Init plus shuffle dominates init at every $(d, w, E \geq 2)$ tuple, typically by $0.01$--$0.03$ nats, with the gap widening at higher $E$.

\paragraph{Bias-variance balance under fixed data.}
The U-shape of the min-loss surface in $(N, L)$ at fixed $E$ is the central qualitative finding of Expt~3.
In the proposed scaling law, the parameter-driven bias term $(N^2 L)^{-r_{\mathrm{param}}}$ \emph{decreases} with model size, while the variance-buildup term $E^{-\delta_E} N^{\delta_N} L^{\delta_L} (s/T)^\delta$ \emph{increases} with model size.
With $df = 0.2$ held fixed (so $P^{-r_P}$ is constant), bigger models pay a larger variance-buildup penalty per ensemble member; ensembling at finite $E$ cannot fully neutralize that growth, so the optimal cell at any fixed $E$ sits at the size where the two terms balance.
Concretely: at $E = 1$ the parameter term has saturated against the data ceiling and additional capacity only buys overfit; at $E = 5$ the variance-buildup floor is partly relaxed but still nonzero, so the medium cell still wins.
This bias-variance balance also explains why the absolute spread of min-val-loss across the grid (a $0.1$-nat band at every $E$) is so much smaller than would be expected from a Chinchilla-style single-pass extrapolation: the parameter term and the variance-buildup term cancel each other across the grid.

% --- 3.3 body ---

The non-monotonicity of $L(t)$ in Figure~\ref{fig:overfit_u_curve} and the power-law $E$-dependence in Figure~\ref{fig:ensemble_scaling} together imply that ensembling at the base size and scaling up a single model are both ways to spend extra compute past the overfit-onset epoch.
Figure~\ref{fig:compute_matched} compares them directly at matched compute and yields the central operational claim of Expt~3: \textbf{at $4\times$ matched compute, ensembling beats single-model scaling by $0.30$--$0.31$ nats, both for width-scaling and (extrapolated) for depth-scaling, in both ensemble strategies}.
Even more striking, the cyan single-model lines are essentially flat or \emph{slightly increasing} in compute --- spending compute on width or depth in this regime does not lower the floor at all, while the same compute spent on members drops the floor monotonically.
The depth axis only reaches $2 \times$ compute (we have $L \leq 24$), but the same direction holds: at $2 \times$ compute, an $E = 2$ ensemble at base size already beats any single deeper model in the grid, and the gap widens at $E \in \{3, 4, 5\}$.
This is the cleanest empirical statement of the bias-variance balance described above: the parameter term has saturated against the data-limited floor, so the variance-buildup term is the only one ensembling actually fights, and it is the only compute channel that still buys real loss reduction.

% --- 3.5: scaling-law fit ---

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.99\linewidth]{experiments/figures/03_compute_matched/expt_fig3_5_scaling_law.pdf}
\caption{Joint fit of the proposed general scaling law to the entire 4 $\times$ 4 grid plus ensembles. (A) Predicted vs observed val loss across all 2000 observations per strategy, color $=$ depth, marker $=$ strategy. (B) Residual vs predicted, colored by $E$. (C) Example cell $d = 24, w = 1536$ (init plus shuffle) --- observed val curves at all $E$ (dots) overlaid with model predictions (solid lines).}
\label{fig:scaling_law}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Notes on Figure~\ref{fig:scaling_law}.}
We fit the augmented form
$$\mathcal{L}(s, N, L, E) \;=\; a\,(s/s_0)^{-r_t} + b\,(N^2 L / k_0)^{-r_{\mathrm{param}}} + c\, E^{-\delta_E} (N/N_0)^{\delta_N} (L/L_0)^{\delta_L} (s/T)^{\delta} + \mathcal{L}_0$$
with reference scales $s_0 = 100$M tokens, $N_0 = 768$, $L_0 = 12$, $k_0 = N_0^2 L_0$ so the reduced variables are $\mathcal{O}(1)$ at the base cell.
The data-limited $P^{-r_P}$ term is absorbed into $\mathcal{L}_0$ since $P$ is held fixed at $df = 0.2$; quantifying $r_P$ is deferred to Expt~4.
We add explicit positive amplitudes $a, b, c$ in front of each non-constant term so that the structural exponents can be identified independently of the units chosen for the reduced variables.
The fit is multi-start nonlinear least-squares on log-residuals --- $\log \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pred}} - \log \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{obs}}$ --- which puts equal relative weight on each observation and lets the variance-buildup term emerge despite being dominated in absolute terms by the early-epoch bias contribution.
Sign constraints: all amplitudes positive, $r_t, r_{\mathrm{param}}, \delta_E, \delta > 0$, and $\delta_N, \delta_L \geq 0$ (the variance-buildup term must grow with $N$ and $L$ to be identifiable as such).
Each strategy is fit separately, with $\delta_E$ pinned at the Expt~2 estimates ($0.88$ for init, $1.09$ for init plus shuffle); free-$\delta_E$ variants are reported in the coefficient CSV.

\paragraph{Fitted exponents (init plus shuffle, $\delta_E$ pinned).}
The fit reaches $R^2 = 0.93$ and per-observation residual $\hat\sigma = 0.030$ on log-loss, with the following structural exponents:
$r_t = 0.41 \pm 0.01$, $r_{\mathrm{param}} = 0.43 \pm 0.10$, $\delta_N = 0.99 \pm 0.04$, $\delta_L = 0.59 \pm 0.03$, $\delta = 2.00$ (upper bound), $\mathcal{L}_0 = 2.96 \pm 0.07$ nats.
The init fit gives qualitatively identical exponents within $1\sigma$ except for $\mathcal{L}_0$, which hits the lower bound of $2.5$ nats.
The two robustly identified exponents are $\delta_N$ and $\delta_L$: variance-buildup grows nearly linearly with width ($\delta_N \approx 1$) and roughly with the square root of depth ($\delta_L \approx 0.6$).

\paragraph{Identification limits.}
Two parameters reach their bounds in at least one of the four fit variants and should be read as conservative estimates rather than measurements.
First, the variance-buildup epoch exponent $\delta$ saturates at the upper bound of $2.0$ in every fit; an unconstrained run prefers $\delta \approx 4$, which is mathematically equivalent to a much smaller fitted amplitude $c$ over the 1--25 epoch range we observe and reflects a degeneracy between $s^{-r_t}$ (decreasing in time) and $(s/T)^\delta$ (increasing in time) that cannot be resolved from this data window.
The proposed conjecture $\delta = 1/2$ is \emph{not} consistent with our grid even at the lower edge of identification --- the U-curve climbs are steeper than $\delta = 1/2$ allows.
Second, $\mathcal{L}_0$ presses against its physical lower bound in the init fit; the model wants more of the loss to live in the variance-buildup and bias-dynamics terms.
Finally, free-$\delta_E$ variants on Expt~3 alone return $\delta_E \approx 0.55$--$0.59$ for both strategies, lower than Expt~2's $0.88$/$1.09$; this disagreement is the price of Expt~3's narrow $E$-range and is why we pin $\delta_E$ from Expt~2 in the headline fits.

% --- closing body ---

The general scaling law in Eq.~(1) of the introduction gathers the four contributions to the loss --- bias dynamics ($s^{-r_t}$), parameter floor ($(N^2 L)^{-r_{\mathrm{param}}}$), data-limited Bayes error ($P^{-r_P}$), and the variance-buildup term that decreases in $E$ and increases in $s, N, L$ --- into a single closed form.
On the Expt~3 grid, $r_P$ is unidentified ($P$ is constant) and $\delta$ is degenerate with $r_t$ over the 25-epoch window, so we report $\delta_N$ and $\delta_L$ as the two clean structural findings: variance-buildup is approximately linear in width ($\delta_N \approx 1$) and grows with depth at a notably slower rate ($\delta_L \approx 0.6$).
The depth-vs-width asymmetry is consistent with the qualitative compute-matched result: scaling depth past $L = 12$ at $w = 768$ does \emph{less} damage than scaling width past $w = 768$ at $L = 12$, because the variance-buildup penalty per added parameter is smaller along the depth axis.
The clean per-strategy $\delta_E$ values from Figure~\ref{fig:ensemble_scaling} (a one-cell, $E$-resolved fit at the base cell with $E$ up to $20$) bracket the theoretical conjecture $\delta_E = 1$, and pinning them as priors lets the Expt~3 grid identify $\delta_N, \delta_L, r_t, r_{\mathrm{param}}, \mathcal{L}_0$ jointly with no remaining strategy ambiguity.
The remaining open question --- whether $\delta = 1/2$ holds at all once we observe more epochs, or whether the variance-buildup term truly grows super-linearly in $s/T$ --- requires longer training or wider $df$ variation, which Expt~4 begins to provide.
